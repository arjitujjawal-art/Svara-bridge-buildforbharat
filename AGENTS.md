## End goal and acceptance scenario

SvaraBridge is a Build for Bharat hackathon prototype for an adult whose speech an unfamiliar listener may struggle to understand and whose hands make typing corrections difficult. The patient uses Android Chrome and speaks Hindi or Hinglish. A receptionist on a separate laptop can listen and respond. A caregiver may assist with first launch, microphone permission, audio setup, and pairing. **Success means the patient completes a correct exchange independently after setup.**

The reference exchange starts when the receptionist offers Friday at five. The patient says “Paanch baje nahi. Monday ko chaar baje milega?” The app must preserve the refusal, privately clarify morning or afternoon, and let the patient hear or inspect the complete counteroffer. Only the patient can authorize delivery. The receptionist hears it on their device and can offer another slot. Confirmation requires the patient to accept that exact offer and the desk to confirm it. Repeat with a judge-selected day or time to prove the exchange works beyond the example.

The original scope was a turn-based in-app conversation about fictional clinic logistics. The PRD prioritizes live patient input, private correction, single-switch access, two-way playback, keyless CPU operation, and a repeatable judge walkthrough. The expanded goal is personal communication after speech impairment. A consenting speaker can use pre-impairment recordings, express an intention through speech or accessible controls, hear the result in a personalized voice and decide whether to use it in the room, with the clinic or in a message. Hindi and English personal playback and an optional one-way outbound telephone adapter are implemented. Native two-way carrier audio, external appointment systems and clinical deployment remain future integrations.

## Personal communication, end to end

1. At `/voice`, the user explicitly consents and selects up to three recordings of their own voice. The browser decodes, downmixes and resamples the beginnings to 3–15 seconds of 24 kHz mono PCM. The API validates consent, type, length and signal before encoding them on the app server.
2. One shared CPU Chatterbox model creates conditioning data specific to the speaker. The server stores four feature arrays and the consent version and time in a per-profile file. The uploaded waveform is temporary. A random access key held by the browser controls synthesis and deletion. Protect the host, browser and disk backups as voice data. There is no account recovery.
3. In the studio, type, dictate in Hindi/Hinglish, or use switch-accessible phrases. Choose Hindi or English output. Listen to the private generated WAV and explicitly approve it before download, device sharing, or an optional outbound call. Editing clears approval. Nearby listeners can hear the audio from the device. The user selects the external messaging recipient in their own app.
4. On a paired clinic call, the same device defaults to its personal voice. Only the patient can attach the reviewed clip to an event, turn, revision and matching text. A successful relay commit makes the retained WAV available to the authenticated desk. The desk's completed playback produces the played receipt. Historical audio expires even if its approved text remains in the room.
5. The optional Twilio adapter allows one configured profile to call one configured number and play an approved clip. The provider receives a ten-minute HTTPS playback grant and announces synthetic speech. The server records submission status for that clip to avoid duplicate calls after an uncertain outcome. Pairing, enrollment and personal synthesis work independently of the phone provider.
6. Deleting a profile removes its conditioning arrays and revokes hosted clips and grants. External copies persist. The browser that enrolled owns the key. The persistent profile volume, temporary clip memory and model cache have separate lifetimes.

## The current call, end to end

1. **Pair the participants.** At `/patient`, **Call my clinic** creates a room and a single-use desk invite. Opening it in a separate browser profile or device pairs `/desk`. The desk sends a date and time in India time. **Try a sample call** instead connects a clearly labeled simulated desk through the same protocol.
2. **Capture the patient’s turn.** An AudioWorklet captures microphone samples. Browser-side Silero VAD measures speech in 512-sample frames at 16 kHz. The endpoint policy retains roughly 300 ms of pre-roll and defaults to a three-second wait. The patient can finish manually. A turn is capped at 30 seconds and submitted as 16 kHz, mono, signed 16-bit PCM WAV. Hang-up and backgrounding disarm capture.
3. **Adjust timing when needed.** Before a call, **Find my pause time** measures three phrases locally. It uses internal pauses of at least 256 ms bounded by sustained speech. With usable gaps in at least two phrases, it suggests 1.8 times the median gap, clamped to 2.5–4 seconds. Applying it stores the wait and a format version on the device. Cancel, manual override, and reset are supported.
4. **Interpret and repair privately.** The recognizer returns raw text for a turn ID and revision. Review binds that turn to the offer open at capture. A limited Hindi/Hinglish grammar checks the refused weekday, hour, or period against that offer. “Monday chaar ya saat baje” retains Monday and presents hour choices followed by AM/PM. A second recording can resolve just the pending hour or period (for example “शाम”). The response is applied only while that revision and offer remain current. Weekdays resolve against the demo week beginning 30 Nov 2026 in `Asia/Kolkata`. Conditional speech such as “haan, lekin…” and unsupported language remain editable in the patient’s own words.
5. **Approve and deliver.** The caption, private Hindi read-back, and outgoing decision derive from one revision. Editing invalidates the previous review. A replaced offer blocks the old structured decision. The patient explicitly approves. The desk receives the committed message and can play it. **Delivered** records peer receipt. **Played** records completed playback.
6. **Complete or decline the appointment.** A refusal or counteroffer closes the old offer for acceptance. The desk can issue a fresh offer. Patient acceptance leaves confirmation pending until the desk confirms the same offer ID. The patient can also send a general message or end the call.
7. **Exercise the accessible and judge paths.** Space selects a highlighted action while switch scanning is enabled. Scan speed and pause controls are available. **Use sample recording** sends a synthetic Hindi WAV through recognition. **Use example transcript** seeds a labeled draft in solo mode. The lab at `/lab` compares the same recording and Silero frame trace at 800 ms, 3,000 ms, and a distinct saved automatic wait when present.

| Voice enrollment, inference and delivery | [voice_engine.py](api/voice_engine.py), [personal.py](api/personal.py), [VoiceStudio.tsx](web/src/VoiceStudio.tsx), [personal.ts](web/src/personal.ts), [model manifest](voice/model.json) | Enrollment requires active consent. Render and preview require the owner's key. Preserve exact audio across approval and desk playback, bounded CPU work, profile revocation and short-lived provider URLs. Keep raw reference audio, keys, and recipient numbers out of logs and Git. |

**Service entry points.** Create and join rooms via `POST /api/rooms` and `POST /api/rooms/join`. Use `POST /api/turns/transcribe` for patient audio, `POST /api/speech` for private or peer playback, and `WS /api/relay` for committed events and receipts. `GET /api/health` reports app, recognition, synthesis, and sample readiness separately. `GET /api/sample.wav` returns the synthetic fixture. `GET /api/voice/status` checks for model assets, `/api/voice/profile` handles enrollment, ownership and deletion, and `/api/voice/renders` creates private previews. See the README for operator phone configuration.

**Operational assumptions.** Patient and desk credentials are distinct. Invites expire after ten minutes. Rooms expire after 30 minutes of inactivity and disappear on process restart. The app bounds room count, event history, command count, audio size, and concurrent speech work. One recognition job can run at a time. Preserve these ownership and concurrency assumptions if changing the worker topology.

## Running and verifying a change

Start a normal local checkout with `docker compose up --build`. The app defaults to host port 8080. Android microphone testing requires a trusted HTTPS origin with WebSocket forwarding. In the managed cloud checkout the app has run on port 3000 using `SVARA_PORT=3000 docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f infra/compose.proxy.yml up --build -d`. The proxy override supplies CA trust. First ASR startup downloads and verifies the 142 MiB multilingual base model into a named volume. The voice-model provisioner verifies about 1.5 GiB of SHA-256-pinned Chatterbox assets separately and exits. Later starts reuse the verified files. Check actual feature readiness after every restart.
